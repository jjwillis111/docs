SoFi: Executive Statements Aimed at Retail Investors

Summary

The public record supports the view that SoFi's leadership, and Anthony Noto in particular, actively cultivates a retail investor following. The record does not show Noto saying in so many words that SoFi is a good stock for individual investors, and nothing surfaced that is directed at Reddit or WallStreetBets by name. The pattern runs through four channels:

1.     Publicized insider buying. Noto makes repeated, relatively small open-market purchases during selloffs, which retail outlets and Reddit amplify as a buy signal. He has tied those purchases to his view that the stock is undervalued.[1]

2.     Retail-facing media. Noto appears on retail investing YouTube channels and podcasts and is a regular guest of Jim Cramer. On one podcast in May 2026 he walked a retail audience through valuation math landing at a $25 to $35 stock, when it traded in the high teens.[2]

3.     Earnings-call messaging. Noto closed the Q2 2026 call by explaining why he buys the stock and predicting the market will eventually "connect the dots."[3]

4.     Product overlap. Many SoFi customers are retail investors on SoFi's own brokerage, and SoFi has built products around democratizing access to IPOs and investing.[4]

The most useful finding for an enforcement lens is a tension between narrative and filings. SoFi has repeatedly emphasized that Noto has never sold a share, while Noto and CFO Chris Lapointe have monetized tens of millions of dollars of stock through prepaid variable forward contracts.[5] Lapointe's public appearances are mostly sell-side conferences, with one exception: a December 2023 retail shareholder Q&A broadcast on Twitter Spaces (Section 2).

1. Publicized insider buying

Noto's open-market purchases are the centerpiece of SoFi's retail story. They are modest relative to his holdings and compensation, but they reliably generate retail coverage and price movement. SoFi's August 2025 8-K states he bought 2,775,307 shares in the open market over the prior four years (the filing reads "2,775,307 million," an apparent typo).[6]

Noto's framing. In a mid-February 2026 interview with Future Investing, a retail YouTube channel, Noto said he viewed SoFi as undervalued and wanted to keep buying, subject to securities law timing rules. He bought roughly $1 million two weeks later.[12] On X, he has written that he "not sold a share even though it's my compensation" because of his confidence in the company.[13]

Retail amplification. A top r/stocks post after the March buys was titled "Anthony Noto bought another $1.5 million of SoFi shares in the past 15 days."[14] The creator who interviewed Noto in February posted the May 8 purchase to roughly 378,000 views, and Barron's and MarketWatch pushed the March 2 purchase on X.[15] There is no evidence Noto personally announces each purchase; the amplification comes from others picking up his reported purchases.

2. Podcasts, videos, and talks

Most significant item: Basis Points (May 22, 2026). Noto spent an hour with two retail-focused stock YouTubers and opened on SoFi's stock price. Per a timestamped summary, he argued the stock's two-times tangible book multiple is a floor, that SoFi deserves a premium to JPMorgan, and that a PEG-based approach gets him to a $25 to $35 stock. He also said he does not see why SoFi cannot be a trillion-dollar company.[16] The stock was around $16 to $18 at the time.[17] This is the closest thing in the record to a CEO giving a retail audience a price target, and the video should be reviewed at the cited timestamps before anyone relies on it.

Jim Cramer. Noto has appeared on Mad Money since at least 2018. Benzinga reports the two have been friends since 1995, and Cramer has repeatedly told viewers he is a buyer of the stock because of Noto.[29]

Social media and audience. SoFi's releases name X and LinkedIn as investor communication channels; whether that covers Noto's personal account is worth checking against SoFi's Regulation FD disclosures.[30] Third-party data suggests individual investors hold roughly 56% to 66% of SOFI, though methodologies conflict.[31] Muddy Waters argued SoFi's low institutional ownership "seems to be a choice."[32]

3. Earnings-call messaging

Noto's most direct stock-price language appears on earnings calls. Closing the Q2 2026 call, he said he is often asked why he buys the stock: SoFi will reach a 20% to 30% return on tangible common equity, and it is "just a matter of when, not if" the market connects the dots.[33] Retail accounts on X circulated the line widely.[34] On the same call he framed SoFi's goal as giving everyday investors access to opportunities they have not historically had, citing its SpaceX IPO offering and an ETF built from the 50 most widely held stocks in SoFi brokerage accounts.[35]

The same August 2025 8-K that disclosed the $24.1 million PVF told investors Noto "has not sold any Company Common Stock since joining the Company in early 2018."[38] Muddy Waters put combined Noto and Lapointe PVF proceeds at $58.3 million and dismissed Noto's $500,000 March 2026 purchase as a token gesture.[39] The two 8-Ks above total about $35.9 million; The remainder of the Muddy Waters figure is NTR. SoFi called the short report inaccurate and designed to deceive, and later chose not to rebut its specific allegations.[40] Muddy Waters has a financial interest, so its characterizations should be weighed accordingly. Separately, SoFi sold stock at $27.50 in December 2025, and shares later fell more than 40% below that price.[41]

5. Context: retail products, the SPAC, and the CFO

SoFi went public in June 2021 through a SPAC run by Chamath Palihapitiya, then a prominent figure among retail traders. Weeks earlier SoFi let members buy IPO shares at the offering price, which Noto pitched as making investing more accessible.[42] This history is context rather than evidence of stock promotion, but SoFi's app reaches millions of retail investors, which could make the line between promoting its products and promoting its stock worth examining.

Apart from the 2023 Q&A, Lapointe's record consists of sell-side fireside chats and scripted earnings-call remarks.[43] The items touching him are the November 2025 PVF and a Muddy Waters allegation, which SoFi disputes, that he misled investors by describing a $312 million loan transfer as a sale.[44] If the CFO concern rests on something specific, the 2023 Q&A recording is the most likely source.
